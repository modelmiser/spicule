# The Seam Is Load-Bearing: A Structured Impossibility Argument for Seamless Safe Systems Languages, with a Constructive Map of What You Can Build Instead

Framing: result-first (the seam is load-bearing), method-grounded (the cold gauntlet earns the claims), with the reflexive builder-calibration observation offered as a hypothesis, not a measurement. **Epistemic status, stated once and meant throughout:** the central impossibility claim is established *by survived calibrated adversarial challenge*, not by a mechanized proof; where we write "the theorem" we mean the precise statement of that argument, not a discharged lemma (see §2 and §6). ## Abstract

We ask whether one programming language can be, at once, *decidable and proof-free* in the common case, *seamless* (no foreign-function boundary between "proof-land" and "systems-land"), and able to give garbage-collector-free memory safety *derived* from its own proof layer, at zero runtime cost, on bare metal. We argue no — not by a mechanized proof, but by an adversarial process of *cold*, context-stripped LLM instances (a proposer, five single-angle attackers, a judge, a synthesizer) that settles a design only after three consecutive survived challenges, and that we *calibrate* three ways: it settles a known-good design, rejects known-garbage, and still fails the target. Across five design questions and 24 rounds, nothing settled; the six recorded incompatibilities are facets of one structured impossibility argument: at every ownership-state merge point the entailment a checker must decide is undecidable, and the seamless/derived/zero-cost requirements each delete the boundary that could relocate that obligation. The argument is non-trivial only under an empirical premise the gauntlet supplies but does not prove — that a seamless/derived/zero-cost system must *accept* the hard shared-mutable weak-memory idioms rather than reject them as Rust safely does. Existing languages ship by conceding *exactly one* property.

We then take the productive concession — make the boundary a first-class in-language **seam** — and find it obeys a **Seam Trilemma**: pick two of {sound re-import, decidable-proof-free-zero-cost safe fragment, seam concentration}. To map it, we *build and break* 37 real Rust seam primitives. Whether a seam closes losslessly is a function of the precondition's *shape*, not of "seams"; the resulting cost-space is a *small, apparently-closed basis* (nine independent axes, an inseparable category, two costs outside the axes) onto which dangers project; the whole result is **domain-parameterized** (memory safety / IFC / liveness), and each domain closes only to the degree its *floor matches the precondition* — a **graded** match. Finally, an observation available only to this warm-builder/cold-adversary shape, offered as a hypothesis: the cold gauntlet appears to *surface* a stable miscalibration in the warm builder, caught eight times along a non-monotonic arc. We are explicit that this is "survived adversarial challenge" from a single model family — calibrated, and machine-checked only in a deliberately *trivial* skeleton (Lean 4) — not a mechanized end-to-end proof, and §6 bounds what that licenses.

## 1. Introduction

A recurring dream in language design is a single language that needs no seam: the specification *is* the type system *is* the compiler; memory safety is *derived* from a proof layer rather than hardcoded; that proof layer erases at zero cost; and the whole thing checks decidably, with no hand-written proofs in the common case, on bare metal. Concretely, the seed for this work asked for seven properties together: (1) executable specification, (2) a unified proof/systems layer with no FFI seam, (3) GC-free ownership *derived* from the proof layer, (4) decidable proof-free checking, (5) bare-metal targeting, (6) zero-cost erasure of the proof layer, and (7) AI-legible semantics.

The seed's own success criterion was *not* "build the language." It was a precise map of which combinations of the seven are genuinely incompatible — established by survived adversarial challenge rather than asserted by authority. This paper is that map, in two halves: a negative half (the seven are not jointly satisfiable, and the obstruction is precise) and a constructive one (having conceded exactly what must be conceded, *here is what you can build, and what everything else costs*).

The framing that makes the negative half land is **covering versus intersection**. A handful of languages *together* already provide all seven properties; but no single language holds all seven, because each concedes one to stay coherent. The union is a covering; no point in the union is the intersection. The substantive question is not "can we list languages that each have some property" — trivially yes — but *why the covering cannot be collapsed into one language.* Our answer is that the collapse *is* property (2), seamlessness itself: "combine them into one seamless language" is the seamlessness requirement, and seamlessness is one of the three things the argument says you must give up. The act of merging is the forbidden move. (We flag in §6 that this identification risks making part of the claim true by definition; the empirical content is that the *other* two concessions do not suffice.)

**Contributions.**
- **C1 (§2).** A single impossibility *argument* unifying six independently-recorded property incompatibilities, with the reduction skeleton that makes decidability the universal antagonist — and an explicit statement of the empirical premise it rests on.
- **C2 (§3).** A *calibrated* adversarial-LLM methodology for design-space impossibility claims — the cold-instance mechanism, and the three controls that make a 0/N result diagnostic rather than ambiguous, together with an honest account (§6) of the controls it still lacks.
- **C3 (§4).** The **Seam Trilemma**: the structural cost of conceding seamlessness, obtained cleanly via a decontamination step.
- **C4 (§5).** A **build-and-break field guide** of 37 seam primitives — a curated, buildable map of where Rust's floor carries versus drops each precondition — organized by a cost-basis that appears closed (nine axes), domain-parameterized, with a graded and non-monotonically-liftable floor-domain match.
- **C5 (§5.5).** A **reflexive hypothesis**: a cold stateless adversary appears to *surface* a warm stateful builder's calibration — a use of the method that, if it survives the controls §6 calls for, neither party could produce alone.

### 1.1 Vocabulary

Six terms carry the paper; each is defined once here, with the running Rust example, so the dense passages downstream are decodable on first read.

- **Floor.** The fixed set of properties the safe language already checks *for free*, with no seam crossing. In our constructive phase the floor is shipped Rust: affine ownership, NLL lifetimes, and decidable trait bounds. Everything is measured relative to this floor.
- **Seam.** A dangerous primitive (an allocator, an atomic, a lock-free queue, an MMIO register) re-exported as a *checked safe interface*, so that ordinary safe code can use it without itself writing `unsafe`.
- **Precondition, and its *shape*.** The set of facts that must hold for the dangerous primitive to be safe. Its *shape* is which conjuncts those are and, for each, whether the floor's vocabulary can *name* it (an affine token, a lifetime, a trait bound) or cannot.
- **Horn.** One of the three things the Seam Trilemma (§4) says you cannot keep all of: (1) **soundness** of re-import, (2) **decidability** (a proof-free zero-cost safe fragment), (3) **concentration** (ordinary code never crosses the seam). A verdict names which horn a seam pays or drops.
- **Concentration.** Horn 3: the seam's obligation stays inside the foundational primitive; ordinary code composes through the safe interface without crossing. It later un-bundles into two independent sub-costs we name *seam-width* (how often code crosses) and *API-locality* (whether the witness infects consumer type signatures).
- **Floor-domain match.** How much of a *soundness domain*'s precondition (memory safety, information flow, liveness) the floor's vocabulary can carry. A *graded* quantity — full, partial, or none — and the central variable of the constructive half.

## 2. The Impossibility Argument

**Status.** What follows is an argument established by survived calibrated adversarial challenge, not a mechanized theorem, and it rests on one empirical premise we state plainly because the whole argument turns on it. Soundness never requires completeness: a sound type system may safely *reject* hard programs, and Rust does exactly that — it is sound, decidable, proof-free, GC-free, and zero-cost, and it rejects mountains of safe code behind `unsafe`. So the argument is non-trivial *only* under the premise that a *seamless, derived, zero-cost* system must **accept** the hard shared-mutable weak-memory idioms — not quarantine them. That premise is supported by the gauntlet (every attempt to design such acceptance failed the same way, six times) but is not itself proved; it is the load-bearing empirical claim, and a reader who rejects it rejects the argument. We call the unified statement below "the theorem" as a name for its precise form, not as a claim of mechanized proof.

We ran the adversarial process (§3) on the seven-property target as five falsifiable design questions — a sequential ownership core, the decidability boundary, proof erasure, the unsafe boundary, and interior mutability under weak memory (the last bundling a sixth, memory-question, incompatibility). Across 24 rounds, **nothing settled.** Every question exhausted its revisions, and the synthesizer recorded a hard incompatibility for each; all six place decidability (#4) on one side of the collision.

| Question | Incompatibility |
|---|---|
| Sequential ownership core | derived ownership ✕ decidable Rust-cheap checking — at the control-flow merge point |
| Decidability boundary | derived *sound* ownership ✕ decidable proof-free checking — on real data structures |
| Proof erasure | zero-cost erasure needs witnesses *irrelevant* to equality; decidable seam-free checking needs them *relevant* |
| Unsafe boundary | a decidable auto-discharged seam can't restore the open, entangled invariant set soundness demands |
| Interior mutability & weak memory | decidable proof-free ✕ sound zero-cost typing of shared-mutable / weak-memory state |
| (bundled memory question) | decidable ✕ no-seam ownership over weak-memory aliasing |

The failure *reasons* were specific and varied — an admissible-contraction double-free; a non-associative `meet` table laundering conflicting ownership states into `Moved`; poison slipping past a location-indexed seam; an implicit-flow secret laundering; the independent-reads-independent-writes (IRIW) litmus test certifying a consensus the POWER memory model permits to fail. Yet they converge on one thesis.

**The theorem (the six are one).** A meta-synthesis pass found the six are facets of a single statement. In plain terms: *a type-checker can be automatic (decidable, no hand-written proofs) or it can accept-and-prove-safe the hard shared, aliased, weak-memory idioms — but not both, because the safety facts those idioms need are, at the points where the checker must combine them, beyond what any always-terminating check can decide.* Formally:

> No type system is both (a) decidable and proof-free in the common case, and (b) the sole auto-checked discharge site for the substructural, heap-quantified, value-/view-dependent invariants that soundness of GC-free ownership over aliased weak-memory state requires *once such idioms must be accepted* — because at every ownership-state merge point (CFG join, loop fixpoint, erasure-time equality, unsafe/atomic/DMA seam) deciding entailment over that invariant class is undecidable, while the no-seam, derived-ownership, and zero-cost requirements each remove the only boundary that could move the obligation out of the auto-checked fragment.

The structure is uniform. Every facet bottoms out at a merge point where the checker must decide entailment between two ownership-or-protocol states. Accepting the hard idioms forces those states to be *substructural* (linear, no contraction), *heap-address-quantified* (∀-indexed footprints), and *value-/view-dependent* (transferable protocol invariants). Deciding entailment over that class subsumes a provably undecidable problem — bi-abduction / Hilbert's tenth problem at the join, the Bradley–Manna–Sipma array-property fragment on the footprints, open-term definitional equality at erasure. The decidable, zero-annotation fragment is the dual of that class: finite-height, quantifier-free, first-order, contraction-admitting — provably too weak to name the invariant, and enriching it to name the invariant inherits the undecidable entailment. **We are careful (§6) that this is a plausibility-strong assembly of real reductions, not a constructed reduction with a formal invariant class and a discharged undecidability proof.**

**#4 is the universal antagonist; #2, #3, and #6 are three forcing functions** — three ways of removing the boundary (a trusted seam, a hardcoded analysis, a runtime witness) that could relocate the hard obligation out of the decidable fragment. This is why existing languages survive (§4): each keeps one such boundary. The framing sits among prior type-system no-go results — undecidability of System F type inference (Wells 1994), of ML with polymorphic recursion (Henglein; Kfoury–Tiuryn–Urzyczyn), and Felleisen's expressiveness ordering for "expressible only by escaping the system" arguments — which it generalizes from inference to the *sound-acceptance-at-a-merge-point* setting.

## 3. Method: The Cold Adversarial Gauntlet

A process that settles *nothing* is, from its outputs alone, ambiguous between "the target is impossible" and "the instrument is rigged to fail." The methodological contribution is the machinery that narrows that ambiguity — and §6 is candid about how far it does *not* close it.

**The roles.** The process (implemented as `orchestrator/`) is a pipeline of LLM instances with disjoint jobs. A **proposer** emits one concrete, formal, falsifiable design decision. **Five cold attackers** — ergonomics, soundness, implementation, expressiveness, prior-art — each receive the proposal *only* and try to break it from a single angle. A **judge** rules each attack genuine-flaw / argued-past / tradeoff, then SURVIVED / FAILED / TRADEOFF, against criteria fixed in code. A **synthesizer** distills a question that fails every revision into a recorded property-incompatibility. A **reconciler** checks whether two settled decisions, held together, force a new incompatibility. A **historian** keeps deterministic state.

**Why coldness is the load-bearing trick.** Each role is a fresh `claude -p` subprocess with the ambient context stripped: a replaced system prompt, no settings loaded (so no hooks), no MCP tools, a clean temporary working directory. The reason it matters is adversarial: an attacker that secretly shares the proposer's framing is not an adversary, and warm shared context is such a secret share. Termination is faithful to the seed: a decision settles only after surviving **three consecutive** cold gauntlets, with fresh attackers each time; any FAILED gauntlet feeds the landed attacks back and resets the streak. (Two honest limits, expanded in §6: coldness is *verified* only by a single probe, and the constructive seam phase of §5 runs at a weaker one-gauntlet bar.)

**Calibration — the trust argument.** We tested the process against itself three ways, which is what turns "0/5" from ambiguous into diagnostic.
- *True negative — a known-good design must settle.* Rust's NLL borrow checker, with the hard constraints (#2/#3/#6) waived, run through the identical gauntlet: **SETTLED** — and honestly. Revision 0 FAILED on a real `dropck`/`#[may_dangle]` use-after-free (the process caught an actual bug); revision 1 then survived three consecutive gauntlets, the judge ruling the residual attacks "puncture the author's reconstruction, not the design."
- *True-positive rejection — garbage must fail.* An obviously-unsound non-design ("programmers just don't write bugs," no checker) FAILED all four revisions, the soundness attacker instantly refuting `check = ⊤ ⟹ ∀e. safe(e)` with a well-typed use-after-free.
- *Stronger-proposer stress test.* Was 0/5 merely a weak one-shot proposer? We re-ran the most-precedented question with a **3-shot self-hardening proposer** that critiques and strengthens its own proposal twice before any attacker sees it. It **still FAILED**, on the same fault line. A 1-shot proposer *settled* Rust; a 3-shot proposer *could not settle* the constrained core — so the *constraints*, not proposer weakness, are decisive.

These controls establish *sensitivity* (fails garbage) and *specificity* (settles good design). They do **not**, on their own, establish that 0/N is specific to the *claimed* obstruction rather than to generic over-constraint, nor that the verdict is independent of a single model family's shared priors — the two gaps §6 names as the most important future controls. The closest thing to discriminant validity we do have is the decontamination move of §4, where the recorded obstruction *relocates* rather than vanishing.

## 4. The Constructive Flip: The Seam Trilemma

The negative half says: concede exactly one of #2 (no-seam), #3 (derived ownership), #6 (zero-cost erasure). The most natural concession is #2 — keep one language, but make the boundary between proof-land and systems-land a *first-class, in-language, machine-checked seam* instead of an FFI to a separate prover. Rust + `unsafe` is this shape informally; Verus, F\*/Low\*, Dafny, and RefinedRust make it formal. The live question is *which* calibration, and what its limit is: the aimed-for calibration keeps the safe fragment decidable and proof-free, puts user proofs only at seam crossings, and — the make-or-break constraint — makes the seam **concentrate** into foundational primitives that ordinary code composes through a checked safe interface without itself crossing.

**Two runs, because the first was contaminated.** Conceding #2 while keeping #3 (`seamful`) gave 0/6 with two near-misses — but the run was contaminated: keeping #3 forced the proposer to *derive a borrow checker from scratch*, and it kept shipping almost-right rules with a dropped premise. Several failures read "Rust-parity, sound in spirit, defective as written" — proposer formalization bugs, not proven structure. The honest fix was a controlled re-run conceding #2 *and* #3 (`seamful2`): the safe fragment is *stipulated* as shipped Rust NLL (a known-good checker), so the entire gauntlet bears on the seam. Still 0/6 — but now clean. The recorded obstruction became "sound re-import needs a linearity witness NLL's region-only vocabulary cannot carry." **Removing #3 did not rescue the seam; it isolated the obstruction onto the seam's re-import boundary** — exactly where the `seamful` argument predicted. This *relocation-not-disappearance* is the paper's one genuine discriminant-validity result: the failure has a specific structural home, not a generic one.

**The trilemma.** The decontaminated meta-synthesis states the result as a trilemma. The seam must concentrate on foundational primitives whose true preconditions are semantic heap-, value-, and concurrency-level invariants, expressible only in an undecidable, certificate-free concurrent separation logic (Iris/RustBelt grade). But the safe fragment can receive only a witness that is simultaneously decidable, proof-free, and zero-cost-erasable. The re-import obligation is therefore strictly weaker than the precondition it must certify, and the missing conjunct forces exactly one of three fatal horns:

> **Pick at most two of:**
> 1. **Sound re-import** — crossing back launders no UB into safe code.
> 2. **Decidable, proof-free, zero-cost safe fragment** — ordinary code needs no proofs.
> 3. **Seam concentration** — ordinary code never crosses the seam.
>
> Keep 1+2 → **drop concentration** (the verified-Rust reality: annotations at use sites, not just inside `unsafe`).
> Keep 1+3 → **drop decidability** (Verus/F\*: the witness goes proof-bearing / needs an external prover).
> Keep 2+3 → **drop soundness** (the interface silently weakens the precondition; UB launders into safe code).

Every shipping point is a deliberate choice of which horn to drop: Rust drops concentration informally; Verus/F\*/Creusot drop decidability; the unsound corner is the one to avoid, and the positive-rejection control (§3) shows it is detectable.

| Program | Concedes | Settled | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| `unified` | nothing | 0/5 | Decidability (#4) ✕ {#2, #3, #6} — keeping all seven is impossible |
| `seamful` | #2 | 0/6 | sound re-import is the casualty (contaminated by keeping #3) |
| `seamful2` | #2 + #3 | 0/6 | **the Seam Trilemma**, decontaminated and structural |

**Decontamination as a general method.** The decisive move was handing the proposer the known-good sub-component (Rust NLL) and watching the failure *move, not disappear.* A 0/N adversarial result is only as good as your ability to rule out "the proposer was just weak"; the cleanest way is to give the proposer the strongest available floor and localize what remains.

## 5. Build and Break: 37 Seams

The trilemma is a map, so the next step was to stop arguing in the abstract and build. We changed the harness: instead of a cold instance *proposing* a rule, the (warm, stateful) builder authored a real artifact — a rustc-compiling Rust primitive re-exporting a dangerous core behind a checked-safe interface, plus a Lean instance and a frozen specification — and the cold panel attacked the *artifact.* The floor is fixed: shipped Rust NLL plus affinity plus decidable trait bounds.

**A reporting caveat, up front, because it bounds everything in this section.** Unlike the negative sweep (three-consecutive-survival bar), the seam phase ran by default a *single* cold gauntlet per seam; only the SPSC seam was re-run to the full three-consecutive streak. So a SEAM-HOLDS verdict below means "one cold panel found no break," which is weaker than "no break exists" — and the paper's own `spin_mutex`→`spin_lock` episode (a one-panel HOLD that a second look broke) is a live demonstration of the gap. Verdict *stability* across repeated gauntlets is unmeasured; §6 lists this as a required experiment. Read §5 as a structured field guide whose individual verdicts are single-panel adjudications, not as certified facts.

**The verdict vocabulary.** SEAM-BREAKS: a horn genuinely drops (soundness, decidability, or concentration lost). SEAM-COSTS [X]: no horn drops, but the seam pays a marked cost named in the bracket — *either* horn 3 (a concentration cost) *or* an out-of-horn axis (§5.2). SEAM-HOLDS: no horn drops and no horn-3 cost, though the seam may still pay an out-of-horn axis as a tradeoff. (Thus a seam can HOLD all three horns yet pay, e.g., zero-cost — the horns and the extra axes are tracked separately.)

### 5.1 The headline: closure is a function of precondition shape

Whether a seam closes is **not** a property of "seams." It is a function of the precondition's *shape* — which conjuncts it has, and for each whether the floor can name it: *can every caller-observable conjunct be carried by the floor's vocabulary (affinity, NLL lifetimes, decidable trait bounds), and if not, is the missing conjunct an external axiom, a runtime-checkable fact, or genuinely unrepairable?*

The two poles make this concrete. A single-producer single-consumer (SPSC) ring buffer **SEAM-HOLDS** — the one seam we re-ran to the full three-consecutive-survival bar: every escaping conjunct is floor-expressible (cardinality → affinity, storage-lifetime → an NLL lifetime, payload safety → a decidable `T: Send`), and the genuinely hard fact (the happens-before relation between producer and consumer) leaves no caller-visible residue. An intrusive doubly-linked list **SEAM-BREAKS [horn 1]**: whole-heap aliasing and liveness escape to the caller, and the `GhostCell` brand carries the aliasing but not the membership-versus-storage lifetime — presented a correct token, the rule type-accepts a use-after-free. Same "seam," opposite verdict, because the preconditions have different shapes.

### 5.2 The cost-space is a small, apparently-closed basis onto which dangers project

The central structural finding of the constructive half, stated first as the thesis the rest of the subsection is evidence for: **across the 37 primitives, every cost a seam pays lands on one of a small fixed set of dimensions — nine independent axes, plus an "inseparable" category and two costs that sit outside the axes — and a new danger class, deliberately chosen to look novel, projected cleanly onto that set rather than extending it.** We call the set *apparently closed* (not *proven closed*): it is "we stopped finding new axes across 37 hand-picked examples," an empirical saturation, not a spanning proof (§6). The nine axes are **Sound, Decidable, Concentration, API-locality, Zero-cost (seed #6), AI-legible (seed #7), Bare-metal (seed #5), Unwind-safety, Leak-freedom.**

The evidence is a chain of un-bundlings, each primitive the *witness* that two previously-indistinguishable configurations differ on some axis:

- The **generational slab** keeps all three trilemma horns yet pays **zero-cost (#6)**: a `Copy` key can outlive its slot, so affinity and NLL cannot carry validity, but a per-slot generation-plus-occupancy tag can, *at run time* — `RefCell`'s move.
- The **branded index** un-bundles *concentration itself* into a **code face** (seam-width — O(1) crossings) and a **type face** (**API-locality** — the brand `'id` never appears as a code crossing but infects every consumer type and traps indices inside a `scope` closure). That the `runST` (PLDI'94) / lightweight-monadic-regions (PLPV'06) lineage pays the identical tax in languages *with no seam or `unsafe` at all* proves the type face is not seam-width. The same primitive also surfaces **AI-legibility (#7)**: a brand is a nominal token, not a readable predicate.
- The **refined index** witnesses the #5⟂#7 dissociation — the (brand-free, legible) corner where a refinement predicate `{v : Int | v < len}` escapes its constructor — and un-bundles **horn 2** into *decidability* (the fragment terminates) versus *solver-freedom* (it needs an SMT solver in the trusted loop, the `unified` "keep" horn).
- The **shared-ownership seam** (a minimal `Rc`) recovers **bare-metal (#5)**: dynamic shared ownership needs a runtime refcount manager and thus a global heap — the crate's sole `extern crate alloc`. The judge confirmed the axis but deflated its novelty to Rust's own `core`/`alloc` boundary (RFC 2480).
- The **type-level-proof index** *probed for an eighth axis and failed* — recorded as a result. It is sound but recovers no clean axis: the off-diagonal is confounded (a turbofish type is static-index-only), the cost is encoding-relative (binary type-level numerals erase the `recursion_limit` hit), and it is mislabeled (`Lt` resolution is *linear*; the real concern is terminating resolution, pre-existing). The discipline can refuse.
- **Take-and-replace** confirmed the eighth axis, **unwind-safety**: moving a value out of a `&mut T` and back leaves a duplicate in the gap, and an unwinding panic drags the caller into a double-drop; the sound witness is an abort-bomb. It holds on all seven prior axes and pays on unwind-safety alone — the clean off-diagonal the type-level index lacked. The relabel: the axis is the *exception-safety-guarantee-level*, and abort is this seam's *discharge* of it (a `replace_with` sentinel discharges the same obligation by paying API-surface) — so the axis carries a **discharge menu**, not a fixed cost.
- **Drain-and-yield** confirmed the ninth axis, **leak-freedom**, distinct from unwind-safety: `mem::forget` is *safe*, so a guard whose `Drop` does load-bearing cleanup can be leaked; the sound witness is pre-pessimization (`Vec::drain`'s trick). The off-diagonal is populated *both* ways (take is unwind-sensitive/leak-safe; drain is leak-sensitive/unwind-safe). The thinnest axis, but genuinely distinct.
- The **self-ref cell** probed for a tenth axis, **move-stability**, and returned the most novel verdict: **real but structurally inseparable (N stays 9).** The danger is distinct (no other axis concerns a value's *address*) and soundly handled (`Pin` over a `!Unpin` cell), but it has no isolable cost: `Pin<&Self>` revokes `&mut` (and that revocation *is* the soundness mechanism), and the cost is non-uniform across witnesses (stack-pin spends API-locality; `Box::pin` spends bare-metal). A distinct verdict category, neither clean axis nor confounded non-axis.
- The **validity seam** (a `#[repr(u8)]` enum byte that must be a valid discriminant — UB even without a deref) asked whether the basis is closed, and the panel unanimously ruled it adds *no tenth axis*: every witness spends a pre-existing coordinate. Its own cost is inseparable in the move-stability sense — a *second* such example, making that a recurring category.

Read in both directions, the picture is a *projection*: the `get_unchecked` family shows one danger with many witnesses spending different axes; validity shows many dangers converging on one axis. Appendix B gives the axis table and the pairwise-dissociation witnesses that make each axis independent. The honest coda: each axis is a *pre-existing* named PL boundary; the contribution is their unification under one rubric — *what does the witness cost?* — a question that, across this sample, has finitely many answers.

### 5.3 Domain-parameterization and the graded floor-match

The basis above is the **memory-safety** domain's. The same shape recurs, with a different basis, in every soundness domain — and *which* seams close depends on how well the floor matches the domain's precondition.

- A **constant-time secret** steps outside memory safety: branching on a secret leaks it via timing — *not* UB. It **SEAM-BREAKS [horn 1, IFC sense]**: `ct_eq(a, b).expose_bool()` then `if b` leaks a relationship; declassification is *etiquette, not type-structure*, and closing it needs whole-program undecidable IFC. So "the cost-basis is closed" refines to **closed per domain.**
- A **capability-gated declassification** probes whether the IFC domain has a *closing* seam (the IFC analog of SPSC). It also breaks, unanimously: `map(self, f)` must hand the closure the *bare plaintext*, which a captured-`&mut` closure exfiltrates with no declassify site; and the affine "budget" counts *uses*, not *bits*. What it *can* enforce — release *location* — projects onto the memory-safety affine axis; the genuinely-IFC coordinate is the *amount* (QIF, Smith min-entropy), which an affine floor cannot bound. **Both IFC seams break for one reason: Rust's affine floor carries none of the IFC precondition.**
- **Type-level lock ordering** steps into a third domain, **liveness** (deadlock is neither UB nor a leak), and **SEAM-BREAKS [horn 1]** two independent ways, which *grades* the match. (1) *Granularity*: `Floor::root` is re-entrant, so two sibling lineages let safe code type a low-then-high inversion — the discipline is per-token while the precondition is per-thread-global. (2) *Coverage*: even patched, the dynamic-acyclicity fragment (runtime-ordered / same-rank locks) escapes the static floor. So the floor-domain match is **graded** (full / partial / none); liveness on a static-order floor is a *partial* match.
- A **runtime lock registry** asks whether *upgrading* the floor lifts the grade. It fixes coverage and pays the predicted price (bare-metal, zero-cost, a prevention→detection modality), but **granularity recurs one rung finer**: `HELD` is keyed on the OS *thread* while async's unit of control is the *task*, so work-stealing migration launders an undetected inversion. The grade is **not monotonically liftable** — the granularity axis is a containment-scope *ladder* (token < thread < task) that no ambient Rust floor tops out.

### 5.4 Closing the map: composite preconditions, and why there is no fourth domain

The remaining twenty seams close the account without *extending the basis* — no new horn, axis, or domain — though several are themselves soundness breaks that raise the broken tally within this block.

**Liveness has exactly three coordinates** — completion, leak-freedom, and progress — each appearing on both a *lock* face and a *session* face. The session family (binary and multiparty session types, choice, delegation, recursion) mapped the progress and completion coordinates; a scoped continuation-passing combinator showed **completion is concentrable after all** (per-endpoint, via an unforgeable return-type obligation rather than a forgettable destructor — so `mem::forget` is defanged), correcting an earlier over-claim that completion was irreducible. Only *termination* remains an undecidable NONE; even guardedness, first mis-filed as undecidable, concentrates behind a sealed `Guarded` marker.

**Four probes for a fourth soundness domain, four deflations.** Convergence (a CRDT) *folds* onto an application-correctness basis; worst-case execution time *breaks* on the cost/cycles gap; totality (integer division) *folds*; and DRF *reclassifies* into memory safety. Four candidate domains, four distinct mechanisms (fold / break / fold / reclassify), none opening a fourth soundness domain — so the domain count is **three, apparently closed**, with an application-correctness *basis* absorbing semantic-law dangers.

**The DRF reclassification collapses a trichotomy to a dichotomy.** A first data-race probe broke by design and *refuted* the claim that DRF is "floor-closed." A correctly-bounded core-only spinlock (after one broken intermediate) **SEAM-HOLDS**, carrying an `unsafe impl` whose obligation is *discharged* by the lock invariant. So the prior implicit trichotomy — IFC closes nothing / aliasing concentrates / concurrency is floor-closed — collapses to a **dichotomy.** IFC is the lone *floor-closes-nothing* domain; **memory-aliasing and DRF are the same** floor-propagates-but-concentrating shape, each with a **static** end (SPSC, the slab — compiler-verified) and a **dynamic** end (`SpinLock`, `RefCell` — a runtime witness). An `unsafe impl` is a proof obligation either **discharged** or **false** (RustBelt semantic type soundness, floor-relativized — a conceptual reading, not a mechanized result here). The adjudication vocabulary itself closed at **nine verdict modes** (a distinct closure from the nine-axis basis of §5.2 — coincidentally also nine) over a **ten-seam** broken tally.

The unifying structural claim: a floor matches a *composite* precondition **component-wise.** A session-typed channel is the first out-of-memory-safety probe to *hold with a cost* — protocol order closes via affine typestate, while completion is a horn-3 concentration cost. *Which horn a danger threatens is set by how the re-import rule packages it* — but that packaging dial is itself **bounded** by whether soundness can be decoupled from the danger. When the data is *owned*, the danger can be downgraded to a mere cost. When it is borrowed across an unwind, address-pinned, or borrow-scoped, the horn is forced and cannot be downgraded.

### 5.5 A reflexive hypothesis: coldness as a possible calibration instrument

We offer this as the freshest observation in the paper *and* as an explicit hypothesis, not a demonstrated measurement — §6 lists the controls it lacks. Across the constructive phase, the cold gauntlet appears to surface a stable, domain-local miscalibration in the (warm, stateful) builder: a recurring tendency we call the **rejection mode** — the seam is *sound*, but the builder's write-up over-claims a horn — driven by a tic to *flee the decidable middle toward whichever extreme is cleaner* (total concentration, or irreducible/global, or undecidable). The gauntlet flagged it **eight** times, and each time refuted the over-claim *from the builder's own citations.*

The apparent arc was **non-monotonic**: horn-deciding early; receding to a marked tradeoff below the horn-deciding threshold as the builder learned to pre-concede the uncarriable half; then re-firing at full strength on the fresh concurrency domain, where a mutex's four auto-trait obligations only got fully enumerated *under the gauntlet* — the builder discharged three and dropped the guard's `T: Sync`, the omission recurred *inside the very salvage meant to prove it fixed*, and a third attempt enumerated all four and held. (That fix is verbatim Ralf Jung's June-2017 std `MutexGuard`-`Sync` bug and its shipped patch, later proved sound by RustBelt, POPL 2018 — novelty ~nil; the *process* observation, not the fix, is the point.)

The constructive dual of the impossibility half is the **five settled salvage lineages** — four one-step, one two-step (the fix itself first broke). You cannot build a *seamless* language; but against a cold adversary a single *seam* can be **converged** to soundness, and the round-count of that convergence *appears to track* how far the danger sat from what the builder already knew. **We stress the caveats that keep this a hypothesis:** it is n≈8 episodes from a single builder on a single project, with no independent novelty rating and — decisively — **no warm-reviewer control arm**, so "only this method could produce it" is untested; and because builder and reviewer share model weights, "a stable builder trait" is not yet separable from "one model behaving differently under warm versus cold context." If it survives those controls it reframes build-and-break from a QA loop into an epistemics experiment; until then it is a promising anecdote.

## 6. Threats to Validity

We state the bounds prominently, because several of them bear directly on the headline claims.

- **This is not a mechanized proof.** "Survived calibrated adversarial challenge" is a strong empirical result, not a theorem. The undecidability reductions cited in §2 are real, but the *assembly* — including the load-bearing premise that the hard idioms must be *accepted* — is a plausibility argument, not a constructed reduction with a formalized invariant class. Where the paper says "theorem/proven" it names the argument's form, not a discharged proof.
- **The Lean skeleton is deliberately trivial.** The Seam Trilemma's logical skeleton *is* machine-checked (`proof/SeamGap.lean`, axiom-clean modulo `propext`), but a cold review forced the depth caveat (`proof/REVIEW.md`): the checked lemma is information-theoretically trivial ("a one-field projection cannot recover a two-field predicate"); all PL substance lives in the *unverified prose-level modeling dictionary.* The mechanization forces the faithfulness claim to be stated and attacked; it does not lift the substance out of "survived adversarial challenge."
- **Single model family — the central threat to a *negative* claim.** All roles — proposer, five attackers, judge, synthesizer, and the calibration controls — are one model family. The three controls establish sensitivity and specificity but do **not** rule out a *shared consensus prior* about this exact target ("a seamless dependent systems language is impossible" is corpus folklore), which would emit 0/N regardless of ground truth while leaving all three controls looking correct. Two known LLM-judge biases compound this: **self-preference** (a judge favors same-model generations — Panickssery et al. 2024) and leniency/verbosity/position effects (Zheng et al. 2023, MT-Bench), both biasing the constructive verdicts *toward* SEAM-HOLDS. **The single highest-value future experiment is a foreign-model replication** (GPT/Gemini/open-weights as attackers and judge) of both the 0/N sweep and a sample of the 37 seam verdicts; a foreign panel reproducing them would sharply weaken this threat. The replication *harness* is implemented and turnkey (`orchestrator/foreign_replication.py`): it re-adjudicates the frozen seam specs with a foreign model as every cold role, reusing the identical role prompts so the only changed variable is the model weights, and reports a per-seam agreement rate against the recorded verdicts. At the time of writing only the foreign credential is outstanding, so the number this experiment will report is not yet in this paper — a limitation we flag rather than paper over.
- **Missing discriminant control.** The controls are all consistent with "the instrument fails anything sufficiently constrained." A target impossible for a *different* reason (or genuinely hard-but-possible) should be run to show 0/N is specific to the claimed obstruction; the decontamination move (§4) is the only current evidence of this and stays within one target family.
- **The seam map's one-gauntlet bar.** As disclosed in §5, 36 of 37 seams ran a single cold gauntlet; only SPSC ran the full three-consecutive streak. Verdict stability is unmeasured, and a 1-panel HOLD demonstrably flipped once in our own data. A required experiment: re-run a sample of HOLDS at ≥3 gauntlets and report agreement; relabel unstable HOLDS as "not-broken-in-one-panel."
- **Reproducibility: the model is intentionally unpinned.** The CLI wrapper omits `--model` (inheriting the operator's configured model), sampling is nondeterministic, and transcripts do not record which model produced each call. A reproducer on a different date or plan runs a different model. An archival run should pin the model and record its ID and version in every transcript envelope.
- **Coldness is verified by a single probe** (one attacker asked about unrelated context answered "no"). A repeatable coldness test with a reported pass rate across roles and runs is needed to make the isolation claim quantitative.
- **The reflexive finding is a hypothesis (§5.5), not a measurement** — n≈8, single builder, no warm-reviewer arm, trait-versus-context confound unresolved.
- **Deflationary novelty throughout.** Nearly every axis and held seam maps to a pre-existing named result. The contribution is the *unification and the map*, and the *method*, not new mechanisms — and the method's validity is exactly what the model-family threat bears on.

## 7. Related Work

Every point on the map is somewhere in the literature; the paper's job is to show they are *one* map — and, for the method, to situate it against work it currently under-cites.

**The method: adversarial LLMs, debate, and LLM-as-judge.** The calibrated cold-gauntlet method must be positioned against a body it resembles: AI-safety-via-debate (Irving, Christiano & Amodei 2018); multi-agent debate for reasoning and factuality (Du et al. 2023; Liang et al. 2023); self-critique and self-refinement (Saunders et al. 2022; Madaan et al., *Self-Refine* 2023); Constitutional AI / RLAIF (Bai et al. 2022); red-teaming language models (Perez et al. 2022); and LLM-as-judge with its documented biases (Zheng et al. 2023; self-preference, Panickssery et al. 2024). Our differentiators — *enforced coldness* (context-stripped fresh subprocesses rather than a shared conversation), the *three calibration controls*, the *decontamination* discriminant move, and the *impossibility-claim target* — should be argued against this body; its absence from an earlier draft was a real gap.

**Impossibility and undecidability in type-system design.** The §2 argument generalizes classic no-go results — undecidability of System F type inference (Wells 1994), of ML with polymorphic recursion (Henglein; Kfoury–Tiuryn–Urzyczyn) — and uses Felleisen's expressiveness ordering for the "expressible only by escaping the system" shape.

**Verified-systems-Rust and seam toolchains.** RustBelt (POPL 2018), RefinedRust, Creusot, Aeneas, Prusti, Kani, Verus, F\*/Low\*, and Dafny are the "covering across a seam" that instantiates the trilemma's keep-1+2 and keep-1+3 corners.

**Separation logic and concurrency.** Iris, iRC11, GPS, and RustBelt are the undecidable CSL in which the seam's true precondition lives, and against which §2's reductions are drawn.

**Refinement, capability, and brand types.** Flux and Liquid Haskell (the refined index); `runST` (PLDI'94) and lightweight monadic regions (PLPV'06) (the branded index, and the proof its cost exists without any seam); GhostCell (the intrusive list).

**Exception and leak safety.** Abrahams' basic/strong/nothrow guarantees, C++ `noexcept`, Rust `UnwindSafe`/`catch_unwind`/`Mutex` poisoning (the unwind-safety axis, and RFC #1508, which rejected exactly the `take_mut` primitive on exactly this cost); the Rust "leakpocalypse," leak amplification, `Vec::drain`, and RFC 1066 / the `thread::scoped` removal (leak-freedom).

**Information flow.** Jif and FlowCaml (the IFC floor the affine floor is *not*); Smith's min-entropy QIF (the *amount* coordinate); CompCert-CT, Jasmin, FaCT (constant-time codegen as *dischargeable*).

**Liveness and deadlock-freedom.** Boyapati–Lee–Rinard (OOPSLA'02) and Kobayashi's level-based analysis; Havender (1968); FreeBSD WITNESS, Linux lockdep, and Abseil (which abandoned static ranks for dynamically-learned graphs — foreclosing the registry design); session types (Honda; Kobayashi's linear-π; Dardha–Gay, FoSSaCS 2018; Scribble / mpstthree) and guarded corecursion (Coq/Agda).

**Positioning.** What is new is not any mechanism but (a) the calibrated adversarial-LLM *method* for impossibility claims — whose scientific validity turns on the foreign-model replication §6 calls for — (b) the unification of scattered axes into one apparently-closed cost-basis under a single rubric, and (c) the reflexive builder-calibration hypothesis.

## 9. Conclusion

A few languages, connected by seams, genuinely cover the whole job; the seam that resists full fusion is *doing real work.* The job takes about one deliberate concession, a seam is where you pay it, and the seam is **load-bearing** — so "you cannot combine them completely, and that's fine" is not a limitation you tolerate but the design win, and it is where verified-Rust toolchains already sit.

The full shape of the result, stated as separate claims rather than one sentence. The Seam Trilemma is a domain-general tension. Each soundness domain has its own cost-basis. A floor closes a domain's seams only as far as it carries that domain's precondition — a *graded* match: full for memory safety, partial for liveness, none for information flow. That grade cannot be lifted for free: upgrading the floor only trades one gap for a finer one and spends the closed cost-basis. A floor can fail a precondition not only by domain but by *structural logic* — affine "at most once" versus linear "exactly once." And a single composite precondition can be matched component-wise, one horn closing while another costs, with which horn a danger threatens set by how the re-import rule packages it — though that packaging dial is itself bounded by whether soundness can be decoupled from the danger.

Finally, the method may offer one thing beyond its findings, and we put it as a question the community can test rather than a claim we have proved: a warm builder against a cold adversary looks less like a QA loop than an *epistemics experiment*, in which the round-count of convergence appears to track the distance between a danger and what the builder already knew. Whether that survives a foreign-model replication and a warm-reviewer control (§6) is, to us, the most interesting open question the project raises.
