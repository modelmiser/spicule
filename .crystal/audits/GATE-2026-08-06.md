## The three that moved most

### AdjudicatorReconciliation — CIRCUMSTANTIAL (was: unguarded/critical)

**Contract**: when two cold adjudicators disagree, a stated rule decides what is recorded.

**Enforcement**: none — the step is a manual coding pass.

**Alternative** — *and it is better than the audit's reading.* The audit treated judge
`outcome` and classifier `SEAM-*` as two measurements of one quantity, then found they
disagree 28% of the time, always toward non-break. But they answer **different questions**.
`roles.py:180-198` (judge) rules on any-angle "genuine flaw," explicitly including
ergonomics, expressiveness, and prior art, and never mentions the horns. `roles.py:366`
(classifier) is horn-only and is told to disregard vote counts. A gauntlet that FAILS on
expressiveness grounds while the classifier records `SEAM-COSTS [horn3]` is not two
adjudicators disagreeing — it is one instrument answering a question the other was not
asked.

Under that reading the operative rule is simply *"Appendix A records the classifier's horn
verdict,"* which accounts for 10 of 11 cases mechanically, plus one disclosed override
(`crdt` r28, `PAPER.md:167`, with a stated rationale). n=1 override is not a pattern, and
"the more lenient decides" over-fits it.

**What distinguishes the readings**: nothing in the data I have. Both predict the same 11
events. The audit's version would need either an undisclosed override going the *other*
way, or evidence the two rubrics were intended as redundant measurements. I found neither.

**Falsification**: nothing executes; no downstream consumer reads the reconciliation.

**Verdict: CIRCUMSTANTIAL.** The defensible residue is narrow and still worth fixing:
(a) the horn-classifier role is never introduced in §3 (verified: 2 occurrences in
PAPER.md, both in §5.4/App A), so a methods-section reader believes Appendix A holds judge
verdicts; (b) Appendix A does not mark which verdicts came from judge-FAILED gauntlets.
Both are disclosure gaps. The 28% figure should not be reported as an inter-adjudicator
error rate — it is largely a construct difference, exactly as the assumption lens
independently argued (its finding FF).

### ClassifierVocabularyClosure — DECORATIVE (was: unguarded/critical)

**Contract**: the instrument's answer space is wide enough to contain the claimed finding.

**Enforcement**: none — `roles.py:396-399` does constrain the vocabulary. That part is true.

**Alternative**: the finding requires the paper to *claim* "all breaks are horn-1 breaks"
as a structural result. **It does not appear to.** Grepping PAPER.md for a universal
statement of that form returns nothing; the paper labels individual seams `[horn 1]` and
never asserts the universality as a finding. If "break" is *defined* as a soundness break
(UB laundering), then breaks being horn-1 is analytic and the paper treating it as
definitional is correct, not circular.

**Falsification**: nothing breaks. There is no claim to be wrong.

**Verdict: DECORATIVE.** This describes the apparatus without constraining anything the
paper says. The audit inherited the framing ("§5 and Appendix A present this as a
structural finding") from a lens without checking the paper — a same-provenance error of
exactly the kind this repo's DEVLOG already names. Recorded rather than dropped, per
`grading.md`: a candidate with no consequence is a source of false confidence in the audit.

### LeanVerdictPolarity — CIRCUMSTANTIAL (was: unguarded/critical, "contradiction")

**Enforcement**: none — nothing stops a `_factors` lemma sharing a `_closes` statement.

**Alternative** — decisive on the framing: **nothing in the Lean file is false.**
`Psynccell := V p` genuinely does factor through `V`, so `sync_cell_datarace_factors` is a
*true* theorem about a trivial precondition. The audit called this a "polarity inversion"
and a "contradiction," implying the kernel asserts something the paper denies. It doesn't.
It asserts something true and uninformative, under a name that suggests a distinction the
statement does not make.

That is a real defect — but it is over-claim plus misleading nomenclature, not
prose-contradicts-code. The warp-types comparison the audit drew is wrong: there, a derive
falsified a stated theorem's hypothesis. Here no hypothesis is falsified.

**What survives, sharply**: §6 names only `_closes` and never mentions `_factors`, so a
§6-only reader cannot learn the convention exists; and §4 advertises
`seam_closes_iff_factors` as "the dividing line" while all 36 named instantiations land on
one side of it. Both checkable, both real.

**Falsification**: no execution path. A reader forms a false belief; nothing breaks.

**Verdict: CIRCUMSTANTIAL** — worth fixing, wrong severity. The 1150-lines-of-apparent-
mechanization point stands on its own.

## The four strongest survivors

**GuardSyncBound — GROUNDED.** Enforcement absent; falsification demonstrated by
compilation (`assert_sync::<Guard<Cell<i32>>>()` builds against `--features lockdep`).
Alternative considered and rejected: *"the seam is already recorded as BREAKS, so this
changes no count"* — true for the tally, false for the attribution. It moves the cause from
liveness/granularity to horn-1 memory safety, and it falsifies §5.5's "a third attempt
enumerated all four and held" for every lineage except spin_lock. This is the only finding
in the audit that is UB reachable from `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]` code.

**SeamRecordProvenance — GROUNDED.** Verified directly: Appendix A files MMIO as
*"COSTS/TCB-axiom (physical fact, outside the horns)"* while
`design_seam/REPORT_SEAM_SPEC_mmio.md` records `SEAM-COSTS [horn3-concentration]`; same
shape for `self-ref cell` and `validity`, both *"TRADEOFF (inseparable)"* in Appendix A.
Alternative considered: *"Appendix A is a deliberate re-coding layer, not a copy"* — which
is legitimate, **except** that Appendix A claims to be "complete… so the §5.4 break census
is checkable from this appendix alone." If it is a re-coding, the census is checkable only
against the re-coding. That is the sharp point, and it is stronger than the audit's.

**NonLlmOracle — GROUNDED.** Zero Miri, zero loom, verified. Alternative considered: *"the
seams are models, and Miri would check the model rather than the claim"* — weak, because
horn 1 is a claim about what actual safe Rust can reach, and the artifacts are actual Rust.

**ForeignControlBias — GROUNDED.** Both biases are in the source and readable: the sample
list at `foreign_replication.py:54-63`, the `"?"` asymmetry at `:200-201`. No alternative

## Ghost facets

**GF1 — the `_closes`/`_factors` convention** (absence-as-safety). Readers are expected to
recover hold-vs-break from an identifier suffix. Nothing checks that a `_factors` lemma
differs from a `_closes` lemma; `lean SeamGap.lean` exits 0 either way. Risk: the
convention is load-bearing for anyone auditing the mechanization and is enforced by
nothing. Recommendation: **formalize** (a `SeamVerdict` carried in the statement) or
**eliminate** (drop the suffix distinction and stop implying it).

**GF2 — which artifact is authoritative for a verdict** (convention-then-reliance).
`design_seam/REPORT_SEAM_SPEC_*.md` is authoritative for verdicts; Appendix A is
authoritative for the census; neither states which wins on conflict, and they now conflict
in at least three seams. `foreign_replication.py:149` silently picks the reports.
Recommendation: **formalize** — state the precedence, then generate one from the other.

**GF3 — `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]` across all 37 test files** (convention-then-reliance).
This is the load-bearing assertion of the entire experiment — the consumer cannot cheat —
and it is re-typed 37 times with nothing verifying all 37 carry it. Currently 37/37 do.
Recommendation: **test** — one CI assertion over the directory.

## Chains

**C1 — ghost-in-chain (highest priority).** The eleven-seam break census (a real, cited,
load-bearing result) depends on **GF2**, an undocumented precedence rule between two
artifacts that disagree. A grounded claim resting on an unwritten convention: the census
is exactly as reliable as a rule nobody wrote down. This is the same shape as the 10→11
off-by-one, one level up, and it is the audit's most durable finding after GuardSyncBound.

**C2 — convention cascade.** §5.5's reflexive-tic narrative (presented as a settled
three-step resolution) rests on the spin_lock lineage, which is genuinely fixed. But the
*class* of defect is enforced only by the builder having remembered it — and
`lock_registry` shows the memory failed. A strong narrative resting on a convention-grade
mechanism.

**C3 — single-root fragility.** Every horn verdict in the corpus roots in the classifier's
closed vocabulary (`roles.py:387-395`). The vocabulary is fine as a definition (see the
DECORATIVE verdict above), but it means the nine-axis basis, the domain count, and the
horn attribution all share one unexamined instrument. Not a defect; a correlated-failure
surface worth naming in §6.

## Note on this gate

Three of the four downgrades came from checking the *paper* against a claim a lens made
*about* the paper. In each case the lens asserted the paper claims something, the audit
inherited it, and the paper does not claim it. That is same-provenance error propagation
inside the audit itself — the failure mode this repo documented on its own arithmetic, now
reproduced by its auditor. The gate exists for exactly this and caught three instances;
whether it caught all of them is unknown.
